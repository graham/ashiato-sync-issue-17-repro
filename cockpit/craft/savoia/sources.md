# Porco Rosso's "Savoia S.21" visual reference

The runtime exterior is an original procedural model made from Godot primitives
(`objects/vehicles/savoia_airframe.gd`). It contains no downloaded mesh, photograph, texture,
trademark, film frame or studio artwork. Nothing from the film or from the model kits below is
saved in this repository: they were looked at, measured with a ruler, and left where they are.

## Which aeroplane this is, which is not a detail

**The model is the aeroplane in the 1992 film, not the aeroplane the film names.** The two share
a name and almost nothing else, and anybody who scales one against the other's numbers will be
wrong by a metre.

| | the real SIAI S.21 (1921) | the film's "Savoia S.21" | this model |
|---|---|---|---|
| wings | **biplane**, the upper wing shorter than the lower | **monoplane**, a straight shoulder wing on struts | the film's |
| propeller | **four-bladed PUSHER**, behind an engine slung between the wings | **two-bladed TRACTOR**, ahead of an engine nacelle on struts over the wing | the film's |
| length, span | 7.62 m, 7.69 m [WP-S21] | about 8.6 m, 10.32 m [KIT, PLAN] | the film's |
| engine | Ansaldo San Giorgio 4E-14, 224 kW (300 hp) [WP-S21] | a Fiat V-12 in the late version (the kits call it an A.S.2) with two copper radiator drums | the film's look; **power a deliberate deviation, see below** |
| crew | one | one (a second person squeezes in behind the pilot in the film) | **one seat** |
| guns | none | two machine guns in the nose | the film's |
| floats | small stabilising floats under the lower wing | one teardrop float under each wing | the film's |
| number built | one, withdrawn before the 1921 Schneider race | -- | -- |

Wikipedia's S.21 article, citing Helen McCarthy's *Hayao Miyazaki: Master of Japanese
Animation* (1999, p. 164), says the film's aeroplane "does not closely resemble the real S.21,
and more closely resembles the Macchi M.33". The M.33 photographs bear that out: a wooden
single-seat flying boat racer of 1925 with a cantilever shoulder wing, a stabilising float under
each wing, and its engine in a streamlined nacelle on struts over the hull driving a two-bladed
tractor propeller. **So the model is built on the M.33's engineering and wears the film's
proportions and paint.** Where the two differ, the film wins, because the user asked for "as much
like the pictures as you can find" and ranks looking right and flying well above exact figures
(`craft_standard.md`).

**If you search for references, search for BOTH names.** Photographs of the real S.21 are captioned
"S.21"; almost everything else captioned "Savoia S.21" on the web is the film's aeroplane or a kit
of it; and the M.33, which is the nearest real aeroplane to what you will be modelling, is found
only under its own name.

## How the numbers were taken

Tags as `modelling_here.md` section 3 asks. Every MEASURED number here is good to **about 0.1 m
and no better**: these are perspective photographs of a 21.5 cm model, not drawings, and a
kit is itself somebody's reading of an animated film.

- **[PLAN] scale: 119.5 px a metre**, from the span, tip 245 px to tip 1478 px = 1,233 px for
  10.32 m. The hull's bottom is nearer the camera than the wing, so the hull's widths read up to
  a few per cent wide; nothing was corrected for it.
- **[SIDE] scale: 109 px a metre** (at 2x), from the hull's nose (x 1032) to its tail (x 115) set to
  the plan's hull length, 8.41 m. The nose is at the right, the keel on y 385.
- **The cross-check neither was scaled to: the step.** The plan puts the end of the wide
  planing bottom 4.00 m aft of the nose (y 662); the side puts the end of the tan forebody 4.05 m
  aft (x 590). Two photographs of two different kits by two different builders, 5 cm apart.
- `tests/savoia_shot.gd`'s orthographic side silhouette at 100 px a metre was laid over [SIDE],
  mirrored and scaled by 100/109 at the nose and keel, with no fitting: hull, step, float, nacelle,
  struts and fin all lie within a few pixels of the photograph's
  (`~/godotgames-drafts/2026-09-17/cockpit-savoia/research/overlay_side.png`, NOT in the repo
  because it contains the kit photograph).

## The deviations, on purpose

- **The pilot sits tall.** The film draws Porco low in the cockpit with the rim at his shoulders
  and his head just over the wing. The wing stands 0.24 m over the hull and the nacelle 0.50 m
  over the wing, so an eye low enough for that rim is level with the wing: a VR pilot would look
  into its trailing edge (the first build, at 1.88 m, did nearly that). The eye is 1.98 m over the
  keel, 0.26 m over the wing's top and 0.24 m under the nacelle's belly, and the seat hangs 1.35 m
  under it (`CockpitStation.EYE_HEIGHT`). The coaming is raised on a hump to 1.46 m round it, and the
  rim is still at the pilot's chest.
- **Very high power**, the user's instruction: "a hot-rod racer". The real S.21 had 300 hp and
  the M.33 450; the film's Fiat is drawn as a racing V-12. What the flying kind carries is decided
  with the handling lane in step 3, and will be written here with the measurement.
- **One seat**, as the real S.21 and M.33 had one, and as the film's has one (a second person rides
  squeezed behind the seat, which is not a station).
- **No crest and no lettering.** The kits carry a personal emblem on the fin and lettering on the
  hull; they are the film's artwork and are not drawn.

## What stays authoritative whatever the model does

Until a `Sim.Kind` exists, `SavoiaAirframe.DRAFT_GEOMETRY` holds what the kind should carry:
half-extents 0.63 x 1.63 x 4.315 m (beam, keel to nacelle top, hull nose to rudder), half-span
5.16 m, 1,450 kg (ESTIMATE, the M.33's 1,255 kg gross plus guns and a bigger engine), one seat.
When the kind exists the simulation owns them, and `tests/savoia.gd`'s clock goes red until the
draft is deleted.

## What would settle what is estimated

- **A drawing.** No three-view of the film's aeroplane with a free licence was found. A published
  kit instruction sheet at a stated scale would replace every [PLAN] and [SIDE] figure above.
- **A front view that is not a close-up perspective**: [FRONT] is taken from about fourteen
  full-scale metres, so the propeller, nearest the lens, reads magnified. The nacelle's width, the
  propeller's diameter and the wing's height over the hull are the three figures carried by the
  least evidence, and the propeller is the one people will look at hardest.
