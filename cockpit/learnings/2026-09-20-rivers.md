# lane/rivers, 2026-09-20 — shores, a river in a canyon, and a quality dial

The user asked five things in one paragraph: find out whether the rivers and lakes we have
gaps for actually work; make the ground and water read differently at a shore; give us canyons
200–300 m deep with a 50 m river in them and a mountain either side, because that is what a
fighter hides in; let a river be placed through a mountain pass, because it would make building
maps easier; and let the mountains' poly count be turned up and down.

Everything below is a measurement or a thing that cost time.

## The survey answered a question nobody had asked out loud

The first deliverable was an ANSWER, not a build, and it was worth the hour. **There are no
rivers in the game at all** — the C++ ground function makes a coast, ridged ranges, valleys,
lakes, towns and airport pads, and no watercourse of any kind. The only thing named "River" in
the repository was a prop in the combat reel's scene. So the "gaps for rivers" were literal.

**The lakes were real and correct** — generated in C++, drawn since 2026-09-17, held to the
millimetre by `tests/water_surfaces.gd`. And they had a bug that had shipped for three days.

## Thirty of fifty-four lakes were being given the open sea's swell

`WaterSurface.dressed` handed every water surface `land_half = Terrain.WORLD_HALF`, 7,200 m,
which is the **island** level's half-width — on every level. Both ocean shaders fade their
vertex displacement in over `max(|x|,|z|) - land_half` from 40 m to 260 m. The lakes exist only
on the generated levels, which put them wherever the hash puts them: out to 19 km.

Measured by `tests/rivers_survey.gd`: **30 of 54 lakes stood outside that band at a motion
factor of 1.00 of full.** Alpine's westernmost is 27 m above the sea and 15.3 km out, and it
heaved with the open swell while `Terrain.water_height` — what a hull floats on — stayed flat.

Both `lake_sheets.gd` and `sea_surface.gdshaderinc` carried a doc block saying a lake is inside
the coast band and so still. **That claim was true on the island and false everywhere a lake
actually is.** A doc block is not a check.

**The fix is that inland water says it is inland**, rather than being inferred from a radius.
Withholding the swell would not have been enough: `ocean_fine.gdshader`'s `swell_steepness`
defaults to 0.15 *in the shader file*, so a lake on FINE chopped with nothing handed to it.

### The probe first passed having measured nothing

`JSON.parse_string` returns every number as a float, `GroundField.configure` rightly refuses
`"world_half is not an int"`, and all five levels came back with an empty catalogue — which the
loop reported as zero lakes outside the band. **RESULT=PASS, 0 lakes.**

This is the tautology trap wearing its other face: not a check that cannot fail, but a check
that passed because it measured nothing. Every survey-shaped check now fails on an empty
result.

## Looking at it moved the whole shore plan

The land_half fix was green and the sea was unchanged, so the work looked done. Then a
photograph of a lake from the air:

- the water was the ocean's near-black `deep_colour` sitting in green grass — a tar pit in a
  field;
- **the sheet's outline was a visible staircase of 16 m squares**, which I had not predicted
  from the code at all;
- the rim the ground function raises round every lake read as a grey concrete kerb.

All three were green in every suite, because an outline is exactly what a headless suite cannot
see. Rule 2 earned its place again.

### The shore, and the numbers either side of it

**The waterline is solved, not snapped.** `LakeSheets` drew a whole cell if any one corner was
wet. Each cell is now clipped to where the ground function's depth crosses zero, with crossings
shared between the two cells either side of an edge — worked out per cell they differ in the
last bit and leave a crack of daylight down every boundary.

Counting vertices standing over ground higher than their own lake's level by more than 0.35 m:

| level | the 16 m grid | the solved edge |
|---|---|---|
| adriatic | 6.88 % | 0.26 % |
| alpine | 7.78 % | 0.19 % |
| gliders | 11.02 % | 0.00 % |
| testfield | 7.70 % | 0.11 % |
| trace | 7.54 % | 0.17 % |

Worst single overshoot 96 m before, 36 m after.

**The depth had to be a float, and the vertex colour was the wrong place for it.**
`ARRAY_COLOR` is eight bits a channel, so every depth over 1 m clamped and the whole grading
collapsed into the first metre. Normalising would have fitted at ~10 quantisation steps across
a 0.45 m shore band, which is how a smooth shallow reads as contour rings. `ARRAY_CUSTOM0` with
`ARRAY_CUSTOM_R_FLOAT` is one float32 and exact.

**The sand band was keyed to sea level written as a number.** `smoothstep(1.5, 5.0, height)`
against ABSOLUTE height — and every lake stands between 13 and 126 m, so **not one of them had
a beach**. Against `height - water_level` the sea keeps exactly the beach it had, because its
level is 0 out there.

## The canyon: two hours spent tuning the wrong number

The first gorge measured **191 m deep against a 260 m ask**. Raising the wall's crest from 1.3
times the depth to 1.6 moved it to 200 m. That 9 m was the clue: **the crest was never the
binding constraint.**

Rock may climb out of a keep-out at `kClearRise`, six fifths of a metre a metre, and no
steeper. A ridge put one depth outside the corridor cannot stand more than 1.2 × that run above
the keep-out's edge whatever crest it is given. Moving the ridge to 1.35 depths out took the
mean to 241 m; 1.6 took it to **305 m with no station short and the shallowest at 214 m**.

**The lesson is not about canyons.** When a change to the obvious knob moves the number by 5 %,
the knob is not the constraint. Find what is before turning it further.

### Rejected, with numbers

- **A keep-out margin to clear the canyon floor.** What stood at the corridor's edge was the
  UNCUT hillside, not rock. Widening the keep-outs by one mountain spacing moved none of it —
  the failing values came back **identical to the metre** — and cost 16 m of mean depth, because
  pushing the talus out lowers the rock everywhere the rim is measured. Identical numbers after
  a change are themselves a measurement: they say you changed something else.
- **Levelling the water at control points.** A level author puts control points kilometres
  apart. Where the ground dipped between two of them the surface stayed on the straight line
  and stood **46 m over the valley floor — a wall of water 50 m wide.** The level is worked out
  every 32 m along each leg now.
- **Walls that stop where the river's line stops.** A `MountainRange` ridge tapers to nothing at
  its last control point, so the rim at the head measured **9 m BELOW the water**. Two depths of
  overrun puts the taper outside the river.

### Concealment was measured, because it was the one thing that could have been assumed

Asked of `SightLine`, which is the game's own answer, over the WHOLE run -- every station of the
river, not one lucky place:

- **100 % of the run is concealed** from the abeam observers 6 km out and 800 m over the rim
  (7.6 degrees of look-down): **0 of 456 sight lines clear**;
- down the canyon's axis 114 of 456 are clear, which is what a canyon cannot help and never has;
- swept upward: 0 % seen at 1.9, 3.8 and 7.6 degrees, 3 % at 14.9, and **43 % at 28.1 degrees**,
  which is where cover goes. Reported, not required, so the angle is a number in the log rather
  than an assumption in a head.

Demanding that all twelve bearings be blocked was asking the geometry for something impossible.
**A check that cannot pass for a true reason is as bad as one that cannot fail for a real one.**

And the control: the same craft on open ground 5 km away is seen from 44 % of the same
observers. Without it, a ray marcher that called everything blocked would pass the concealment
check outright.

## The quality dial could not go where the brief said

The brief said the poly-count dial belongs on `DetailReach` (distance) or `Finish` (PLAIN/FINE),
not on a third axis. **It cannot go on either, and the reason is not a preference.**

The mountains' triangles ARE their collision, static collision is not replicated, and every peer
builds its own. `tests/rock_quality.gd` measures what a per-machine dial would cost: between the
coarsest and finest quality, **3 of 400 sampled points move more than 60 m, the worst by 69.9 m**
— two peers at different settings would stand rock 70 m apart on those faces.

So it is a LEVEL's number, `rock_spacing`, agreed by everyone on the map. Measured:

| spacing | triangles | tiles | build |
|---|---|---|---|
| 96 m | 11,278 | 37 | 7.5 ms |
| 48 m | 41,762 | 37 | 25.6 ms |
| 24 m | 157,398 | 37 | 95.1 ms |

The triangles follow the inverse square (3.70× and 3.77× against the law's 4.00×), the summit
moves 17.2 m across the whole range, and **a tile keeps its 1,920 m** — the tile's size is the
constant and the quad count is derived from it, because a tile is a draw call and a culling unit
and how much world it holds should not change because somebody asked for finer rock.

## I said this project had no line-of-sight code. It had, and it was named for something else.

`tests/canyon.gd` first marched **its own ray** against `Terrain.ground_height` every 20 m,
because a survey for `line_of_sight`, `can_see` and `occlu` found nothing.
**`CockpitWorld.ground_leg_is_clear` had answered exactly that question since the generated
ground landed**, over both the height fields and the mountain triangles, through one shared
max-pyramid. It is named for a FLIGHT LEG, because the autopilots needed it first.

Two lanes ran that survey the same night and both concluded the same wrong thing. A grep for
what a thing is *called* misses a thing named for its first caller. **When a survey says a
capability is absent, ask what it would be called by whoever needed it first.**

The cost of being wrong here was not just duplicated work: a hand-rolled sampler is a **second
opinion about where the ground is**, so a canyon could have hidden a fighter from this test and
not from radar. `SightLine` is now the one answer and the concealment numbers below are its.

### And the bias is one-directional, which is the right way round

The pyramid may call a CLEAR line blocked and never a blocked one clear, at the grain of a 32 m
square. Read as concealment: terrain hides slightly MORE than geometry alone. So a canyon that
measures concealed cannot be exposed in the game by a rounding — design to the nominal depth and
trust it.

### The control caught the broken world, which is what a control is for

The first run with `SightLine` reported **456 of 456 abeam sight lines clear** — including lines
straight through 300 m of rock. The world had not been `start()`ed before `set_ground`, so it
kept no ground, and `SightLine` answered its safe default (TRUE, nothing in the way) for every
line.

**What gave it away was the control also scoring 100 %.** A concealment check on its own would
have read "0 % concealed" as a finding about the canyon. The open-ground control scoring a
*suspiciously perfect* number is what said the instrument was broken rather than the canyon.

## A new test file is a documentation obligation

`tests/docs.gd` fails with `every_test_scene_is_in_suites_txt_or_is_a_named_probe` the moment a
new `.tscn` appears under `tests/`. A suite goes in `suites.txt` with a budget; a probe goes in
`docs.gd`'s `PROBES` dictionary with a sentence saying what it is for. It cost two red runs to
learn, once for `rivers_survey` and once for `canyon_shot`, and it is not obvious from anywhere
else.

Also: paths written in a doc are checked for existence relative to the project, so a repo-root
path like `todo/x.md` must be written **without backticks** — in backticks it is read as a
project path and `res://todo/x.md` does not exist. The existing convention in `world.md` already
does this; it is just never stated.

## Two traps in the tooling

**`build.ps1` without `-Double` prints `ok ashiato_gd.double.dll (bytes)` for a file it did not
build.** Five suites then ran on the double editor against the old library and passed —
including the hash suite, which passes trivially because the old library has none of the change
in it. Caught only because the DLL's mtime was the lane's provisioning time.
**The guard is to ask the library a question only the new code can answer.** The probe asked
`GroundField.configure` for the new key: stock said `problems=[]`, double said
`"unknown key rivers"`.

**`vswhere.exe` is not on a lane's PATH** and the first build dies inside vcvars with
`'vswhere.exe' is not recognized`. Prepend
`C:\Program Files (x86)\Microsoft Visual Studio\Installer`.

## What is not here

- `todo/rivers--canyon-floor-crenellation.md` — a regular crenellation along the canyon floor's
  edge, visible from the air, evidence says it is in the ground and not the rock, three places to
  look with the cheapest first.
- `todo/rivers--shoreline-on-steep-ground.md` — the 0.0–0.26 % tail of lake-shore vertices that
  16 m sampling cannot resolve, and the bisection that would fix it.
- **The lake rim.** The ground function holds the land 2 m above every lake for about 90 m, to
  hide the drawn water's outer edge. The edge is now cut exactly at the waterline, so the rim may
  have no job left, and it is the biggest thing still wrong at a lake shore — it reads as a grey
  kerb. Removing it moves every recorded world hash, so this lane asked the user instead of
  doing it.
