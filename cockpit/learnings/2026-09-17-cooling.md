The brief: *"Model cooling towers with steam (low quality okay), place a couple in the world."*
Landed as `world/cooling_tower.gd` (the model), `world/tower_plume.gd` (the steam),

## 1. The waist is the whole job, and three references that never met agree on it

A natural-draught hyperbolic cooling tower is recognised by exactly one thing: the hyperboloid
of one sheet with its throat about four fifths of the way up. Put it in the middle and it is an
egg-timer. So the model's real content is three numbers, and none of them is typed:

| | |
|---|---|
| throat at **0.8326** of the shell | solved from the envelope below |
| throat at **0.8110** of the shell | Ferrybridge C's four published dimensions, independently |
| throat at **~0.81** | measured off a photograph that never saw either |

**The envelope is Film Cooling Towers Limited's Baglan Bay job** — height 296 ft, ring beam
diameter 244 ft, throat diameter 124 ft, top diameter 131 ft — because it is the only entry in
their record with a COMPLETE and internally consistent set of four from one authority.

**Didcot's line in the same table was rejected, and the reason is the reusable part.** It says
"114 m high 91 m diam" and does not say WHERE the 91 m is measured. Taken as a ring beam
diameter it puts the throat at two thirds of the base, against 0.471 (Ferrybridge C) and 0.508
(Baglan Bay) from the two sources that give a complete set. `modelling_here.md` section 3
already says a published dimension has a datum and that a source which does not state it is not
yet evidence; this is that rule paying for itself on the first model of the day.

## 2. A MEASUREMENT CHOSE A DATUM, which is worth more than the measurement

**This is the finding I would send to another lane.** "Height 296 ft." does not say whether it
is measured from the pond or from the ring beam, and there is no source that settles it. The
photograph does.

The left tower's silhouette in a Commons long-lens elevation of the eight Eggborough towers
(`geograph.org.uk 6003681`, CC BY-SA 2.0) was taken by thresholding sky (252 ± 1) against shell
(78 ± 9) at 200, and its left edge fitted over 299 rows by least squares on `r²` against
`(1, y, y²)` — the exact linear form of a hyperboloid of one sheet, so the fit is closed form
and cannot wander. **rms residual 0.38 px, worst row 1.02 px.** The right edge, fitted
independently over 407 rows, agrees on the throat radius to **0.015 px**.

That gives one dimensionless number, `b / r_throat = 2.4465`, which carries no scale at all —
the tower's base is behind a treeline and its height was never measured. And that is exactly
what makes it decisive:

| reading of "height 296 ft." | implied `b / r_throat` | from the measurement |
|---|---|---|
| **shell, ring beam to lip** | 2.346 | **4.1 %** |
| overall, columns inside it | 2.112 | 13.7 % |
| shell plus columns on top | 2.580 | 5.4 % |

The photograph never saw Baglan Bay and the table never saw Eggborough. **Go looking for a
quantity that is a pure ratio, because a ratio can arbitrate a scale you have no second source
for.** `modelling_here.md` section 3's "two references that never saw each other agreeing on a
third quantity" is usually written as a way to CHECK a number; used the other way round it
chooses between readings of a sentence.

**One pixel there is worth 0.19 m**, so nothing off that photograph is better than about 0.2 m
— ample for a shape ratio, useless for a fitting. Say it where the number is used. And the
camera is **3.6° off level**, measured from the rim ellipse (7 px deep against a 112 px
semi-major), so vertical foreshortening is 0.2 % — evaluated rather than trusted.

**The overlay is `screenshots/2026-09-17/cockpit-cooling-06-*`, and it is the evidence.** The
brief warned that a poor fit of the right thing and a good fit of the wrong thing read the same
in a residual. Here the fitted curve traces the silhouette from the lip to the treeline in red,
and its extrapolation to the base — ground the photograph never shows — is drawn in orange
DASHES with that said in the caption. A reader can dispute that picture.

**And the whole measurement is reproducible by a script that does not read the write-up**
(`~/godotgames-drafts/2026-09-17/cockpit-cooling/research/measure_tower.py`). Running it
audited the write-up and corrected three figures I had rounded by hand: the throat-radius
agreement between the two edges (0.01 → 0.015 px), the measured ratio (2.4467 → 2.4465) and
`r_top / r_throat` (1.0847 → 1.0785, because I had taken the rim's far edge as the top of the
shell instead of where the outer silhouette settles). **None of the three changed a conclusion,
and that is the point: the script is how you find out whether they would have.**

## 3. Two mutants found the SUITE rather than the tower

Six mutants, each restored by rewriting the whole file, each asserted to apply exactly once.
Four landed where they should. Two did not, and both are general.

### The winding check was a tautology, and a tower turned inside out printed PASS

`_wound_outwards` is copied from `tests/ship_models.gd` and asks whether each face's winding
agrees with the normal stored beside it. **The builder chooses that normal FROM that winding**,
so the two can only ever agree. Flipping one comparison in `CoolingTower._face` turned all 840
triangles inside out and the suite printed `RESULT=PASS`.

This is `modelling_here.md`'s deepest rule — a check that shares its subject's frame of
reference cannot catch the frame being wrong — met in the place it is easiest to walk into,
because the check was **copied from a suite where it is not a tautology**. In `ship_models` the
meshes come from several builders with different conventions; here one function writes both
halves. **A check copied between files can change from a check into a restatement without a
character of it changing.**

What catches it is anchored on the object, not the file: the shell is convex about the tower's
axis, so no face of it may look inwards.

### And the first version of THAT check fired on every mutant, which is as useless as none

It asked the whole mesh. A raked column is a prism whose inner face genuinely looks at the
axis, and so does the pond wall from inside, so one face in six was "inward" whatever the
builder did. **A check that goes red on every mutant is not a strict check, it is a broken
one**, and a mutant sweep is how you find out which you have — a single-mutant "red before fix"
would have shown it red and looked like success.

Above the ring beam the only thing drawn is the shell (the legs stop at `COLUMN_HEIGHT` exactly
and the pond wall is 2.2 m tall), so the shell is selected by a **height rather than a label**.

### A band cannot separate two readings that both land near it

The doc block claimed the 10 % band on the photograph chose the datum. It rejects one wrong
reading (13.7 %) and not the other (5.4 %) — a mutant that made exactly that change left the
check green. The Arleigh Burke's hull depth again. The typed figure is checked as well now, and
it is the figure that fails. **Print the band for the reader and assert the figure.**

## 5. A camera bar has to be the question its own picture exists to answer

The first framing check was one share-of-frame number for both views, and it could not be
satisfied. **At 75 degrees of vertical field you cannot have the whole of a 278 m plume in
frame AND the towers filling a third of it.** One number had to be either loose enough for the
wide view or impossible for it, and the temptation — the exact thing the brief warned about —
is to loosen it until it passes and call it a budget.

Both views now require every tower corner inside the frame and none behind the lens. Then the
close view, which exists to show the shape, requires the towers to stand 0.30 of the frame's
height (0.473 measured); and the wide view, which exists to show the station, requires the top
of the steam to be in frame too. **Each bar is the thing its view is for**, and the stand-off is
worked out rather than tried: a subject `h` tall fits at `h / (2 tan 37.5°) = 0.65 h`.

## 7. The budget is tied to a thing somebody measured

The brief's rule: a budget nothing can fail is not a budget, and a limit that is not the thing
it protects is the mirror of it.

A tower is **840 triangles in one surface** at 20 sides and 11 bands. The limit is 850 and one
surface, and what it protects is named in the check: two towers is 1,680 triangles in two
draws, **against the F/A-18F exterior's measured 1,700** — so the pair of them costs less than
one fighter. It currently sits at 99 % of its limit, which is the point.

**How many the world can hold**: at 840 triangles and one draw each, the aircraft first-LOD
budget of 100,000 triangles and 20 draws would take about 20 towers before the DRAW calls ran
out, not the triangles. A tower is draw-bound, not triangle-bound, so the number to watch when
somebody wants eight of them at a station is the draw count, and they should be a MultiMesh at
that point rather than 8 MeshInstance3Ds.

## 8. Smaller things worth keeping

- **`Terrain.WIND` is `Vector3.ZERO`**, by the user's own 2026-09-15 ruling ("no wind"). The
  plume leans on the same authority and with the same arithmetic as `FireYard.set_drift`, so it
  stands straight up in the game and the lean is exercised only by the unit check. That is the
  right way round: when wind arrives, the steam and the smoke lean together without anybody
  touching either.
- **`wisp.gdshader` was already the right shader for steam** and no new one was written. It is
  unshaded, mix-blended, two-sided, alpha-thinned towards the silhouette, faded at the bottom,
  mist-included and handed the mist's own clock — four `tests/lint.gd` rules that a second
  shader would have been a second place to get wrong. **Before writing a see-through shader
  here, read `world/shaders/` for one that already passes lint.**
- **A ring has to sit ON the throat.** Walking the hyperbola's parameter in equal steps put the
  narrowest DRAWN ring 1.6 m above the waist the envelope solves to — 0.8507 against 0.8326 —
  so the model's own crease was in a different place from the number it is judged on, with
  every headline check green. `ring_heights` shares its bands either side of a ring at `t = 0`.
- **A prism square to a raked leg changes the model's bounds.** Column legs with a cross-section
  square to their rake put their corners 0.31 m under the pond and 0.31 m over the ring beam,
  making the drawn model 0.6 m taller than the tower it claims to be. Sheared prisms with a
  horizontal cross-section draw the same and are checkable: bounds are 0.00 to 99.22 m.
- **Placement asks the town, not a map.** `PowerStation.sites()` reads `TownCatalogue.towns()`
  and offsets from the seated centre, so the station moves with its town when the generated
  ground seats it somewhere else. A typed pair of coordinates would put two 99 m towers in the
  sea the first time the seed changed, with every check green.

## 9. The `Plating` experiment: 31,000 faces inside out, every winding check green

Team-lead traced my tautology finding out of my lane and into `Plating`, the shared helper
almost every model in this game is built through, and asked for the measurement. Here it is.

Mutant: `Plating.facing`'s one comparison flipped, so every quad placed by where it faces is
wound **inwards**.

| suite | result with every model inside out |
|---|---|
| `hangar` | **PASS** — 0 of 8,072 faces wound against their normal |
| `prowler` | **PASS** — 0 of 984 |
| `ship_models` | **PASS** — 0 of 15,750 across carrier, battleship, gunboat, launch, submarine |
| `merchant_models` | 0 of 7,902 on winding — **and red on three FREEBOARD checks** |
| `carrier_shape` | **PASS** |

**Over 31,000 faces across ten models, and not one winding check noticed.** The Arleigh Burke
went onto main today partly on "every face is wound outwards", and that evidence was worth
nothing. `facing` reorders the corners and then calls `quad`, which recomputes the normal from
the *final* winding, so the stored normal is a function of the winding by construction — the
same shape as my own bug, in the file mine was copied from.

**The one suite that went red did so for the wrong reason, and that is a second finding.**
`merchant_models` failed `the_destroyer_freeboard_at_the_stem_is_its_reference` (0.00 m against
8.59 m), `..._amidships_...` (−1.99 m against 5.87 m) and `the_destroyer_sheer_falls_aft`.
**Look at the numbers: the mutant's "transom" reads 5.87 m, which is the reference's
amidships.** They are the reference values rotated by one station. Reversing a quad's corner
order does not move a single vertex — the point set is identical — so those checks are not
measuring positions: **they read the vertex buffer by INDEX.**

So the only thing in the fleet that noticed a wholesale winding flip noticed it by accident, and
a check that reads vertices by index will equally go red on a retessellation that changes
nothing measurable — which is exactly the change it should be silent about. **Two opposite
failure modes, one mutant, in one suite.**

I fixed nothing; `plating.gd` is restored byte-identical and the repair is three suites and nine
models.

### And the mutant runner refused twice before it applied

Both refusals were traps `modelling_here.md` already lists, and **both would have produced the
same all-green output as the real result.** On this experiment in particular, a silent
non-application would have led me to report the opposite conclusion with total confidence.

1. My anchor's indentation was **spaces where the file has tabs**.
2. `plating.gd` is **CRLF and my anchor was LF**. The doc lists five occurrences of that across
   three lanes.

**"Assert the anchor is present exactly once" is not hygiene, it is the whole safety of the
method** — it turned two silent false-negatives into two loud refusals. And a third trap, from
my own memory rather than the doc: writing the runner through the Bash tool collapsed `\r\n`
into a real newline, so the script would not parse. `chr(92)` is the way round it.

## 10. A duration is not a rate, and comparing the two would have been wrong

Team-lead pointed at the best reference point in the project: `FireYard.RISE_SECONDS` is 38 s
with the comment *"slow enough to read as smoke rather than steam"*. **Somebody has already
decided, in writing, where that line is.** I wanted the other side of it.

**But 38 s is for a 260 m column and my plume is 179 m, so the two durations are not
comparable.** A duration is a height over a rate; holding one against the other compares two
different heights and says nothing. The comparable quantity is metres a second:

| | rate |
|---|---|
| `FireYard`'s fine column | 260 / 38 = **6.8 m/s** — the speed tuned to read as smoke |
| my plume at the 22 s I first wrote | 179 / 22 = 8.1 m/s — **19 % faster, which is not visibly anything** |
| my plume now, at 12 s | 179 / 12 = **14.9 m/s — 2.2× the smoke rate** |

`the_steam_climbs_faster_than_the_rate_tuned_to_read_as_smoke` asks both sides of their own
file's constants, so neither can move without it noticing.

**And the picture settled it better than the arithmetic.**
`cockpit-cooling-07-steam-at-2.2x-the-smoke-rate-beside-a-real-fires-column.png` has one of the
island's hillside fires in the same frame, 692 m away. The fire's column is grey and drifting;
the towers' plumes are white and dense. **Two rising columns side by side in one frame, reading
as two different substances, is a stronger claim than any number** — and it was free, because
the fire was already there.

## 11. The budget question, answered: this is a tripwire and it says so

Team-lead asked whether 850 protects something or is a number I landed near — the `hitch`
failure mode, where a limit is not the thing it guards. **He was right to ask, and the answer
turned out to be that the tower is not triangle-bound at all.**

A tower is 840 triangles in **one surface**. Against the first-LOD budget of 100,000 triangles
and 20 draw calls: **119 towers fit the triangles and 20 exhaust the draws.** The constraint on
"how many towers can the world hold" is the draw count, by a factor of six. So the useful thing
to write down for whoever wants an eight-tower station is *past a handful they must become one
MultiMesh*, and the triangle count is not what will stop them.

So 850 is kept and relabelled: **a tripwire against somebody quietly subdividing the shell**
(20 sides → 32 puts it at 1,344 and it goes red — mutant run, it does), explicitly **not** a
frame budget. "Less than one fighter" is a rhetorical anchor; a pair of static towers and a
flying fighter are not interchangeable loads. **The real constraint, what a frame costs with the
towers in it, is UNMEASURED**, and the file names the probe that would measure it
(`tests/scenery_shot.gd` with a view at the station).

**A tripwire and a budget are different things and the difference belongs in the file**, because
a tripwire mistaken for a budget is exactly how `hitch` ends up red all day at 0.599 ms against
0.55 while the thing it guards is 8.33 ms.

## 12. Two answers `lane/buildings` needs

**The tower builds in isolation and needs no level.** `PowerStation.one()` returns a finished
`Node3D` — shell, material and plume — from statics alone: no `Sim`, no spawn, no `Terrain`, no
town. `sites()` is the part that needs a world and a gallery never calls it. `_one_tower` now
builds through `one()` too and only adds what a *place* gives it, so the gallery and the world
cannot drift apart.

**The plume needs ZERO settling frames, and that is worth knowing rather than assuming.** A puff's
phase is `index / PUFFS` plus the clock, so at `t = 0` all twenty-four are already spread up the
whole column. **It is not an emitter** — `puff_at` is a pure function of its clock, and the plume
at `t = 0` is the same plume as at any other moment.

The check states that as a *stationary property* rather than as "it looks fine at zero": the
puffs span 0.000 to 0.958 of the column at `t = 0`, **and** the plume's total strength varies
**1.20 %** across a whole cycle. A plume that filled in over a few seconds would pass the first
half of that and fail the second. A later change that turns this into something needing a warm-up
cannot do it quietly.

## 14. The towers are solid, and the check that proves it is the only one that could

Team-lead confirmed nobody held `world/terrain.gd`, so the gap from item 1 of What's next is
closed: `PowerStation.boxes()` hands `Terrain.boxes()` **33 static boxes a tower, 66 for the
station, 6.5 % on top of the island's 1,014** (`tests/world_map.gd` for that figure).

### Which way an approximation is allowed to be wrong is a design decision, not a detail

A static box has no rotation and a hyperboloid is not one, so the solid is an approximation. The
question that decided its shape was **which direction the error may point**:

- **Inwards** — a box inside the shell — means a pilot clips the skin before the collision
  registers. Ugly, bounded, and **assertable**: "no box corner is outside the drawn mesh" is a
  thing a check can hold against the vertices.
- **Outwards** — a box proud of the shell — is an invisible wall in open air. Worse to fly into,
  and **unfindable**: "the invisible wall is not too bad" is not a property.

So the shape was picked by measuring both errors rather than by choosing a number:

| | boxes | into the shell | outside the drawn 20-gon |
|---|---|---|---|
| one inscribed square a band | 11 | 14.18 m | 0.000 m |
| one "equal error" square a band | 11 | 9.74 m | **5.690 m** ← rejected |
| three boxes a band, all at 45° | 33 | 12.81 m | 0.000 m |
| **three boxes a band, 22.5/45/67.5°** | **33** | **11.01 m** | **0.000 m** ← chosen |

### Cut the solid on the DRAWN rings, or the check has to interpolate

The first version cut the solid on heights of its own choosing, which meant a band end could fall
between two drawn rings — so the check had to compare a box against a **chord** of the drawn mesh,
and a chord under a convex curve sags by about 0.17 m here. **That is the same size as the thing
being checked.** Cut on `ring_heights()` there is nothing to interpolate: a band's radius *is* a
drawn ring's radius, and the check compares boxes to vertices with no arithmetic in between. It
also means a retessellation moves the solid with the shape, for free.

**And the radii are the polygon's, not the circle's.** What is drawn is a 20-sided prism whose
flats lie `cos(π/20)` inside the circle — 1.23 %, or 0.46 m at the ring beam. A box fitted to the
circle pokes through its own flats by that much, and the mutant that removes the allowance turns
the check red.

## 15. Two bugs in one check, and only flying found either

Everything above proves boxes of the right shape are in the right list. **None of it proves the
simulation does anything with them** — and "a Dictionary reached a list" is exactly the shape of
the carriage bug. So a fighter is flown at a tower in a bare `CockpitWorld` given the island's slab
and every box `Terrain.boxes()` lists (`tests/airbase.gd`'s arrangement). It found two things on
its first two runs, and **no other check in this suite moved for either.**

### One: the group was documented and never set

`Terrain.boxes()` reported **0 tower boxes** and the aeroplane went 514 m out the far side. I had
written *"they carry Group.TOWER"* in a comment and never written the line. And it is worse than
uncounted: **`SceneryYard.draw_boxes` reads `int(box["group"])`, a missing key is `null`, `int(null)`
is 0, and 0 is `Group.ROCK`** — sixty-six invisible boulders inside two cooling towers.

**A doc block that states a rule with no code enforcing it is the finding, not the documentation**
(`modelling_here.md` §5 says this about `cockpit_shell.gd`). I wrote one into my own file within an
hour of quoting the rule at somebody else.

### Two: the aeroplane flew UNDER the tower, and it looked identical to broken collision

Second run: still 514 m out the far side — **and it was 1.6 m up at the tower's station**, having
sunk 28 m over 320 m, because at 0.6 throttle with no pitch input a fighter descends. It had gone
cleanly through the 9 m column bay the model deliberately leaves open. **The check was reporting
"the towers do not stop a fighter" when what it had measured was a feature.**

> **A check that flies something has to say WHERE THE THING WENT, or its failure cannot be read.**

The height and lateral offset at the tower's own station are printed on every run now, and they are
what turned "the collision is broken" into "the aeroplane was 1.6 m up" in one line. Started 150 m
out at 60 m it reaches the tower at 56.1 m, where the shell is 22.4 m in radius, and **stops at
−29.4 m without ever crossing the axis.**

### And it flies a control, which is the half that makes it able to fail

"The aeroplane did not come out the far side" is also what a spawn that never moved looks like, and
what a world that refused the kind looks like. The same aeroplane on the same heading, offset 220 m,
must pass the tower — it reaches **699 m past**. Without that, the two bugs above would have been
indistinguishable from a third: nothing having been simulated at all.

### Four mutants, all landing

The seam cut out of `Terrain.boxes()`; the boxes fitted to the circle; one box a band instead of
three; and the group left off. **The third is worth noting: a single inscribed square a band does
not merely clip deeper on paper — the fighter gets through.** The cross of three is load-bearing,
not decorative.

## 16. `hitch` was red and it was not me — proved, not assumed

The final full gate: **163 of 164**. The one red was `hitch: the_whole_world_fits_in_a_tick` at
0.602 ms against 0.55. Team-lead had said all day that `hitch` was red on main for growth that
costs nothing — **and I had just added 66 static boxes to the world, and that check times the
world tick.** "It was already red" is exactly the sentence that lets a real regression through.

**First attempt, main against lane, interleaved:** pairs read −0.004, +0.204 and +0.028 ms, and
**main itself failed in the third pair** at 0.656 ms. The spread between pairs was larger than any
effect 66 boxes could have. That is inconclusive, not exoneration — two checkouts differ in more
than the one thing under test.

**The clean experiment is the same checkout with and without the change.** Same DLL, same moment's
machine load, only `Terrain.boxes()` handing over the towers or not; six pairs, interleaved, **order
alternated each pair** so a load arriving mid-pair does not always land on the same side:

| | |
|---|---|
| per-pair difference, with minus without | **median −0.005 ms**, spread −0.175 to +0.038 |
| without the towers | **fails `hitch` in 6 of 6** (0.612 to 0.676 ms) |

**The towers cost the tick nothing measurable, and the world is red on this machine without them.**
That is also the expected physics: a static box is not stepped, it only enters the broadphase near
a dynamic body, and the station stands 700 m from the nearest traffic.

**Compare the thing you changed against itself unchanged, not against somebody else's checkout.**
`modelling_here.md` §8 says interleave A/B/A/B under load and judge only the difference; the
addition is that the A and the B must differ in *one* thing, and a mutant runner that swaps a single
line is the cheapest way to arrange that.

## 17. A picture with none of its subject in it, passing the check that it was in the frame

Adding the cooling tower to `tests/buildings_gallery_shot.gd` broke Hangar 03's picture, and the
gallery said PASS.

Every standalone building was stood at the **same** `_a_clear_place()`. That was fine while there was
one of them. With two, a 12.8 m hangar stood inside a 99 m tower's 88 m footprint, and the hangar's
picture was **a frame of grey concrete with no hangar in it anywhere.**
`hangar-03_is_in_the_frame` passed at **0.74**, because it projects the subject's own corners into
the window and asks what share of it they cover — **it cannot tell a subject in view from a subject
behind a wall.** The corners were exactly where they should be. The wall was between them and the lens.

**What gave it away was the caption**: `nearest: Cooling tower, with its steam, 1 m`. Two buildings
one metre apart is not a place, it is a collision. **The neighbour line, added to stop one kind of
misreading, caught a completely different fault** — which is the argument for printing the context
of a measurement even when no check reads it.

Fixed by standing each standalone `STANDALONE_APART` (500 m) further out, past the biggest one's
width plus the furthest any gallery camera stands; `no_standalone_building_is_stood_in_another` is
asked of the places actually chosen rather than the constant. **The deeper hole is unfixed and
named**: the gallery's coverage check measures projected corners, not visibility, and a pixel test
would be the real answer — `modelling_here.md` §7's hangar lid (*"a box's only vertices are its eight
corners"*) is the same shape, and it was the **pixel** that caught that one too.

## 18. A guard born from one confusion that did not look for it

The gallery's doc block says why every picture names its nearest other building: team-lead read a
steam plume off a picture of the train and it was **a fire 1,139 m behind**. So the guard exists for
exactly one confusion — a fire's column read as something the subject is doing — **and it searched
only buildings.**

The first picture of the cooling tower had a fire's grey column standing in frame behind the steam,
and the caption named a hangar 225 m away. **For the one subject in the gallery whose whole point is
a white column rising, that is the worst neighbour to leave unnamed.**

`_fire_in_frame` now names every fire whose base, middle or top — `FireYard` draws a 260 m column
and never culls it — actually lands in the picture, asked of `Sim.fires` (what is drawn) rather than
`Terrain.fires()` (where fires started). **And it found them in five of the nine pictures, not just
mine**: both air base hangars, the revetment and the control tower each had three or four unlabelled
columns about 1.8 km off. Only fires in frame are named; a line that names fires nobody can see
teaches a reader to skip it.

**When you inherit a guard, read what it was written to prevent and check that it looks for that.**
This one had drifted from its own reason in the space of a single file.

## What's next

1. ~~No tower is a thing in the simulation.~~ **Done** — see sections 14 and 15. What remains of
   it: **the column bay is deliberately not solid**, so an aircraft can fly under a tower through
   a 9 m gap, and the check above proves it can because that is how it found the sinking fighter.
   If somebody decides that is wrong, the honest fix is not a box across the bay — that would
   claim solid where twenty raked legs and a lot of air are drawn — but a box per leg, which is
   20 more boxes a tower and probably not worth it.
2. **`COLUMN_HEIGHT` is the only number here with no source.** 9.0 m, an ESTIMATE. No source
   read gives a ring-beam level and the one photograph good enough to measure it on has its
   tower bases behind a treeline. **A photograph with a column bay clear against sky, or any
   published ring-beam level, would settle it** — and it is the number that decides how much
   daylight shows under the shell, which is what a tower is recognised by after the waist.
3. **The 5 % the model does not explain.** The Eggborough silhouette's shape, with the base
   taken at the throat/base ratio the two complete published sets agree on, predicts
   `H / D_base = 1.32`. Didcot's published pair gives 1.253 and Baglan Bay's 1.213. Nothing
   here reconciles them, and the honest reading is that these towers genuinely differ in
   slenderness between stations. A second measurable elevation of a DIFFERENT station would say
   whether that is true or whether the Eggborough reading is out.
4. **One station, one kind of tower.** `PowerStation.STATIONS` takes more rows and
   `TOWERS_EACH` more towers for nothing; an eight-tower station is a line of data. At that
   point read note 7 above — it becomes draw-bound and wants a MultiMesh.
5. **The `Plating` repair is not mine and is the biggest open item on the board.** Three suites,
   nine models, and every "wound outwards" result on main until it is done. Section 9 has the
   measurement and the pattern; `merchant_models`' index-reading freeboard checks want fixing at
   the same time and for the opposite reason.
6. **The gallery's "in the frame" check cannot see a hidden subject** — see section 17. It
   projects corners; it wants a pixel test. And the gallery frames a building's *body*, so the

7. **What a frame costs with the towers in it is unmeasured** — see section 11. A view added to
